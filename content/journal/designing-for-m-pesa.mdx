M-Pesa isn't a payment method in Kenya. It's **the** payment method. If your
checkout treats it as a secondary option behind a credit card form, you've
already lost.

## What this changes

- The phone number field is the most important input on the page.
- Confirmation copy needs to speak in M-Pesa's language — "enter your PIN", not
  "complete transaction".
- Error states must be unambiguous. A failed payment with no clear reason is a
  support ticket waiting to happen.

## What I do about it

Every e-commerce flow I build starts with the M-Pesa happy path, drawn on paper,
before a single component exists. The card form comes second. That order is not
negotiable.

The reason is simple: the field placement, the button copy, the confirmation
screen, the error handling — they all cascade from the primary payment method.
If M-Pesa is the primary method, everything adjusts. If it's secondary, the
whole flow is subtly wrong, and users notice even if they can't articulate why.

## The phone number input

It needs to accept `+254`, `07`, `01`, and the local formats without spaces. It
needs `inputmode="tel"` so phones bring up the dialer keyboard. It needs to
remember the country code so a returning customer types seven digits, not
twelve.

```tsx
<input
  type="tel"
  inputMode="tel"
  autoComplete="tel"
  placeholder="+254 700 000 000"
  className="..."
/>
```

Small things. But they add up to a checkout that feels like it was built for
Kenya, not adapted from somewhere else.

Confirmation copy "Enter your PIN" — not "complete transaction". "Check your
phone for the M-Pesa prompt" — not "awaiting payment confirmation". The user is
holding their phone. Speak to what they're doing, not to what the system calls
it.

Error states Every failure needs a human explanation. "M-Pesa could not be
reached. Try again in a moment." "Your PIN was incorrect. You have two attempts
left." "Your balance is insufficient for this order."

Not "Error 403". Not "Transaction failed". The user will call support if they
can't tell what to do next, and every support call is time you don't get back.
